## Pre-Move Conditions

### Derivatives conditions that actually mattered

The most reliable derivatives tell was exchange risk-parameter escalation. On MEXC and Bitget, abnormal names repeatedly moved from normal funding settlement to hourly settlement, sometimes with caps widened to ±2% or ±3%, while maximum leverage was cut from 50x or 100x down to 20x. That happened in RAVE, SIREN, BULLA, and KAT. These are not cosmetic changes. They are exchange acknowledgements that the perp is decoupling enough from spot, or that liquidation risk is high enough, that the venue must tighten mechanics. In public data, that signal arrived before or alongside the terminal acceleration, not after quiet mean reversion. citeturn34search5turn34search0turn12search4turn12search5turn34search4turn34search6turn30search2turn23search1turn23search2turn30search0turn36search0turn37view0

The next strongest signal was the relationship among price, open interest, and funding. When price rises while shorts still pay extreme negative funding, the move can keep extending because the market is not being advanced by fresh spot demand alone; it is being advanced by a financing burden on trapped shorts. RAVE is the cleanest example publicly available: reports tied the rally to deeply negative funding, heavy short liquidations, and by April 18 CoinGlass data showed open interest had already fallen 83% from its April 11 peak, meaning the squeeze had started converting from new positioning into forced exits. That distinction matters: once OI starts falling into a rising price, late longs are often buying a mature squeeze rather than an early one. citeturn27search5turn10search9turn39view1

For breakdowns, the inverse setup mattered more: price stalls or rolls over while longs are crowded, funding stays positive, and exchange leverage/risk tiers are being tightened. SIREN’s March–April path fits that pattern better than any other name in this set. Public reporting described 56% and 88% collapses that came after leveraged upside extensions, while exchange notices show multiple leverage cuts and funding-interval changes around the same period. In practice, “OI rising” by itself was not predictive. “OI staying high while exchange risk controls tighten and price stops making cleaner highs” was the useful variant. citeturn11search9turn11search5turn34search4turn34search6turn12search4turn12search6

Mark-price mechanics and auto-deleveraging are part of the signal stack, not just execution trivia. Bybit’s documentation states that liquidation is triggered by mark price rather than last trade, precisely because low-liquidity or manipulated prints can deviate from a fair price. MEXC’s funding documentation also notes that funding deductions affect liquidation price, and its ADL documentation shows that high-profit, high-leverage accounts are prioritized for deleveraging when market stress overwhelms normal liquidation. On MEXC specifically, rising ADL-bar risk during a squeeze is not noise; it is evidence that the platform itself is under stress. citeturn32search1turn32search4turn32search6turn32search7turn39view1turn39view2

### Spot, order-book, and on-chain conditions that repeatedly mattered

Across the four case studies, visible spot depth was tiny relative to derivatives notional. BULLA is the most extreme public example: CoinGecko showed only about $7,000 of +2% depth and about $6,000 of -2% depth on MEXC, while CoinGlass showed about $11.2 million of open interest and about $21.1 million of 24-hour futures volume. ## Repeatable Detection Framework

The key lesson from all four cases is that the best edge comes from watching **market structure stress** rather than “bullish setups.” The checklist below is intended to front-run the crowd by prioritizing float, exchange mechanics, and free-float flows.

### Pre-pump checklist

A high-quality pre-pump setup exists when most of the following are true at the same time:

- Effective float is constrained: circulating supply is a small fraction of total supply, or public reporting indicates whales/team clusters dominate effective float. RAVE, BULLA, and KAT clearly met the unlocked-float condition; SIREN met the concentration condition in public reporting. citeturn20view0turn15view0turn17view2turn28search4turn28search7
- The exchange starts tightening mechanics: funding frequency shortens to hourly, caps widen to ±2% or ±3%, risk tiers harden, or max leverage is cut to 20x. citeturn34search5turn34search0turn12search4turn12search6turn30search2turn23search1turn23search2turn36search0
- Futures notional is large relative to visible spot depth, so squeezes can move spot rather than just settle against it. citeturn15view0turn16view2turn19view0turn16view0turn24search1turn17view3
- Funding is negative or increasingly punitive to shorts, but price still holds or stair-steps higher. That means the crowded side is still trapped. citeturn27search5turn39view1turn34search5
- Whale or exchange-flow data suggests free-float contraction: withdrawals from exchange, re-accumulation, or reclaiming of previously distributed supply. citeturn28search2turn28search7turn29search6
- A narrative or listing catalyst improves access only after the structural setup already exists. Treat the catalyst as ignition, not proof of value. citeturn7search5turn28search1turn37view0

### Pre-breakdown checklist

A high-quality pre-breakdown setup exists when the same structural asymmetry is still present, but the flow regime flips:

- Price has already gone vertical and exchange mechanics stay stressed or get tighter. citeturn34search0turn34search5turn34search4turn12search4turn30search2turn23search2
- Funding is positive or longs are crowded after the squeeze, yet price stops making clean highs. SIREN’s repeated post-squeeze collapses fit this regime. citeturn11search9turn11search5turn40search8
- OI stays elevated or stops falling, but spot no longer absorbs. That is often the handoff from “squeeze” to “distribution.” citeturn10search9turn16view0turn24search1
- Team/whale exchange deposits, unlock overhang, or fresh exchange investigations hit after the vertical move. citeturn17view2turn29search7turn29search6turn40search1
- Retail attention arrives after access has broadened and the move already looks obvious. That is usually late, not early. citeturn7search5turn17view2turn20view0

### Scoring system

Use a 0–100 score, but score **structure**, not sentiment.

- **20 points — float asymmetry.** Give full points when circulating/unlocked supply is less than roughly one-third of total, or when credible public analysis says whales control most effective float. citeturn20view0turn15view0turn17view2turn28search4turn28search7
- **20 points — exchange stress response.** Full points when any venue moves funding to hourly, widens caps, cuts leverage to 20x, or hardens risk tiers. citeturn34search5turn34search0turn12search4turn12search6turn30search2turn23search1turn23search2turn36search0
- **15 points — perp dominance over spot.** Full points when futures volume materially exceeds spot or when OI is large relative to market cap in a low-float asset. citeturn16view2turn16view0turn24search1turn24search3turn18search0
- **15 points — depth fragility.** Full points when visible +2%/-2% spot depth on active venues is only in the low five figures while derivatives notional is in the millions or tens of millions. citeturn15view0turn19view0turn17view3
- **15 points — free-float flow change.** Full points when whales withdraw or re-accumulate into the pump, or deposit/distribute into the breakdown. citeturn28search2turn28search7turn29search6turn40search1
- **10 points — funding/OI regime alignment.** For longs, negative funding with price resilience; for shorts, positive funding or crowded longs with price failure. citeturn27search5turn11search9turn11search5
- **5 points — ignition catalyst.** Listing, pre-market conversion, or headline narrative. This matters least because it is often late. citeturn7search5turn37view0turn28search1

Interpretation:
- **0–39:** No real edge. Move is probably ordinary volatility.
- **40–59:** Watchlist only.
- **60–74:** Tactical setup.
- **75–89:** High-conviction squeeze or breakdown watch.
- **90–100:** Only when float asymmetry, exchange stress, and free-float flow change are concurrent.

## MEXC Execution System

On MEXC, a viable high-leverage execution model has to be built around **mark-price risk, funding-driven liquidation drift, and ADL probability**, not around candles alone. MEXC explicitly notes that funding deductions affect liquidation price, and its ADL ranking prioritizes profitable high-leverage positions when the system is stressed. That means a trader can be directionally right and still manage the trade badly if they ignore venue mechanics. citeturn39view1turn39view2

### Earliest entry triggers

For a long, the earliest valid trigger is a Tier 1 structural event while price is still below the obvious blowoff: an hourly-funding switch, a leverage cut, or a pre-market-to-standard conversion **combined** with constrained float and no immediate spot collapse. RAVE on April 12 and KAT on March 18–26 are the clearest public examples. For a short, the earliest valid trigger comes later: after the squeeze is mature, the market is still mechanically stressed, and price fails to extend cleanly despite crowding. BULLA and SIREN both fit that pattern better on the downside than on the upside. citeturn34search0turn34search5turn37view0turn36search0turn23search1turn23search2turn11search5turn40search8

### Confirmation entries

Confirmation should come from structure, not excitement. For longs, confirmation is price holding after the exchange intervention while funding remains hostile to shorts or while free float is still contracting. For shorts, confirmation is price failing after the intervention, with signs that spot is no longer absorbing. In both cases, if the move is already obvious to retail, confirmation is often already late. The exception is a KAT-type case where broad spot breadth, not just perps, is participating. citeturn27search5turn28search7turn16view0turn24search1turn17view2

### Invalidation, stops, and leverage scaling

Stops should be built around mark-price behavior, not just last trade, because liquidation is triggered by mark price on major perp venues and MEXC funding directly alters liquidation distance. If the post-announcement regime fails immediately and mark re-enters the pre-stress range, the thesis is wrong. If the trade is profitable but ADL bars rise toward 3–4, scale down; MEXC’s own rules make that a platform-stress warning. For leverage, the research conclusion is blunt: 20x–35x is the probe regime; 35x–50x only belongs on confirmed continuation with cushion already built; 75x–100x is only rational for micro-duration adds funded by open profit, never for fresh exposure once an exchange has already cut leverage to 20x. citeturn32search1turn32search4turn39view1turn39view2

### Partial profit-taking, reversal exits, and no-trade conditions

Partial profit-taking should start into the first heavy liquidation burst, especially when price still rises but OI begins falling, because that usually means the squeeze is consuming trapped counterparties rather than building durable sponsorship. Exit before reversal when the signal stack stops improving: funding remains extreme but marginal upside shrinks, exchange scrutiny increases, or free-float expansion appears through deposits or unlock overhang. Do nothing when the asset lacks float asymmetry, when books are deep enough to absorb liquidations, when exchange mechanics are normal, or when the only “signal” is a trending feed. The best way to avoid fake squeezes is to require both structural scarcity and spot confirmation. If the pop is purely perp-led and free-float is not getting tighter, there is no asymmetric edge. citeturn10search9turn27search5turn17view2turn29search7turn29search6

## Blindspots and Failure Modes

The biggest false positive is treating exchange risk changes as directional on their own. Venues sometimes cut leverage or alter funding simply because volatility is high, not because a squeeze must continue or a breakdown must start. Those notices are powerful because they identify stress, but they still need to be combined with float asymmetry and flow direction. citeturn34search5turn34search0turn12search4turn30search2turn36search0

The second major trap is fake OI or fake funding interpretation. Rising OI with sideways price can mean balanced long/short buildup rather than a pending explosion. Negative funding can stay negative for longer than traders remain solvent, and positive funding can persist through slow distribution. Funding is a regime descriptor, not an automatic trigger. citeturn39view1turn27search5turn11search9

The third trap is assuming every vertical move is an insider scheme. RAVE has the strongest public manipulation smoke; SIREN and BULLA also show concentrated-float behavior; KAT is much less clear-cut. If a framework cannot distinguish “broad repricing with leverage” from “engineered float squeeze,” it will overtrade and overfit. KAT is the reminder that not every violent move belongs in the same bucket. citeturn29search7turn29search6turn28search7turn26search5turn37view0turn39view0

The fourth trap is survivorship and hindsight bias. These four names are memorable precisely because they became extreme. Many tokens may experience hourly funding or leverage cuts and still fail to produce a tradable move. Likewise, once a chart has gone 10x, every earlier exchange notice looks prophetic. A real system must test whether the signal stack beats a control set of stressed pairs that **did not** explode or collapse. citeturn34search5turn34search0turn12search4turn30search2turn36search0

### Open questions and limitations

Public evidence was strong on tokenomics, exchange notices, visible depth, funding mechanics, liquidation rules, and some whale exchange-flow reporting. It was weak on time-aligned historical CVD, on fully reconstructable venue-by-venue order-book spoofing, and on validated OTC inventory shifts for all four names. So the highest-confidence conclusion is this: the repeatable edge is in **front-running structural stress**, especially float asymmetry plus exchange mechanical tightening, rather than trying to reverse-engineer every microsecond of the tape.
